How the real game works

This is a summary of public guides to the Marketplace bikes game (Venture Strategy and Strategic Management). Your instance may have different numbers, so check the in-game Help File.

Quarter by quarter

QuarterWhat happens
Q1 Set upOrganize the team, name the company, set goals and segment priorities, design brands, order a store and/or web center, buy 3D printers. There are no sales, no hiring and no ads in Q1. Q1 results match your pro forma exactly.
Q2 Test marketPrice, rebates, ad copy, media, website, hiring sales and service staff, pay, demand forecast, production schedule, operating capacity, market research.
Q3 AdjustRead the feedback (Brand, Price and Ad Judgments, sales per salesperson), fix what missed, add a store and capacity.
Q4 InvestWrite the business plan and pitch VCs, use the bank line, and fund R&D for features that arrive in Q5 and Q6. Most auditor limits lift.

Key facts

  • Three segments: Recreation (fun, comfort, price sensitive), Mountain (off-road, durable, handles inclines), Speed (light, fast, aerodynamic, least price sensitive). Willingness to pay is $1,100 / $1,365 / $1,580.
  • Four store cities: New York ($200k setup, $62k/qtr lease), Rio ($90k / $17k), Amsterdam ($136k / $44k), Bangalore ($84k / $13k). The web center is $150k / $60k and sells globally. Stores open one quarter after you order them. (The course deck lists five regions; the guides group Europe with the Middle East and Africa under Amsterdam.)
  • Auditor limits: 1 new store per quarter in Q1 to Q3, max 7 employees per location in Q2 and Q3, max 3 printers per quarter through Q3, ad limits of $80k in Q2 and $160k in Q3, price minus rebate over $400, Q1 price under $2,100.
  • Each printer costs $30k and makes up to 520 bikes per quarter. Production = fixed capacity × operating capacity × worker productivity (about 70% early), plus up to 4 hours of overtime a day at double pay. Economies of scale are big: about 26% cheaper per unit at 250 bikes, 42% at 500 and 49% at 1,000 compared with 100.
  • Demand is salespeople × units per salesperson. That's about 30 to 70 per salesperson in Q2, 75 to 125 in Q3, and 150+ by Q4. A service rep sells about 30% as much. Customers judge brand design, price and ads on a 1 to 100 scale. 70 is the minimum to be considered, 90+ is very good, and a gap of a few points against a rival matters a lot. Ad Judgment over 80 is very good, and more than 6 or 7 claims crowds the ad.
  • Stockouts cut next quarter's demand by half your stockout %, and they also lower Market Performance.
  • Emergency loan: if cash goes negative, the loan shark covers it at 5% to 25% (your deck says 10% to 25%), is repaid next quarter from whatever cash you have, and takes 1 share per $100. Bankruptcy happens when retained earnings plus common stock goes below zero. Guides suggest a pro forma cushion of $300k to $500k.
  • Balanced Scorecard: Total Performance = Financial Performance × Market Performance × Marketing Effectiveness × Investment in Future × Wealth × HR Management × Asset Management × Manufacturing Productivity × Financial Risk. Any factor at zero or below zeroes the total.

Common rookie mistakes

  • Overspending on a hopeful forecast, which leads to an emergency loan and dilution.
  • Too little capacity, which means stockouts. That costs sales now, cuts demand next quarter and lowers Market Performance.
  • Too much operating capacity: idle workers still get paid.
  • Hybrid bikes aimed at two segments that end up mediocre in both.
  • Crowded ads, or ads that don't lead with the segment's top needs.
  • Skipping market research, then being unable to tell why a brand didn't sell.
  • Paying below the industry average, which lowers productivity.
  • Teammates overwriting each other's saved screens. Agree on who edits what.
  • Being too timid. Guides say the winners grow aggressively once Q4 lifts the limits.

Q1 prep checklist

Q1 team decisions are due Sat Oct 24, 11:59 PM PT.

  1. Organize the team, assign roles and agree on who edits which screen.
  2. Name the company.
  3. Read the market info: segment needs, willingness to pay, and potential by city.
  4. Goals and Strategy: pick a primary and secondary segment, and say why.
  5. Design 1 or 2 brands per target segment, each built for that segment only.
  6. Sales Channel: order your first store, and maybe the web center. It opens in Q2.
  7. Manufacturing: order printers for Q2 demand. A rough forecast is salespeople × 50 per salesperson, divided by about 70% productivity.
  8. Accounting: check the pro forma and keep cash for Q2 hiring, leases and production.
  9. Final Check, then Submit.
